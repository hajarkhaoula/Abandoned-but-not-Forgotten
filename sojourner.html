Sojourner Rover

The little explorer that opened the way for future robotic missions to Mars.

01 — INTRODUCTION

The First Little Explorer

Sojourner was NASA's first successful wheeled rover to explore the surface of another planet. It was part of the Mars Pathfinder mission, launched on December 4, 1996. The spacecraft landed on Mars on July 4, 1997, in a region called Ares Vallis. Although small, Sojourner became an important milestone in robotic space exploration.

02 — DESIGN AND TECHNOLOGY

A Small Robot with a Big Mission

Sojourner was a compact robotic vehicle with six wheels. It was approximately 65 cm long, 48 cm wide and weighed about 10.5 kg. It used solar panels and rechargeable batteries to obtain electrical power. Its rocker-bogie suspension system helped it travel across the rocky Martian surface.

03 — SCIENTIFIC DISCOVERIES

Exploring Martian Rocks

Sojourner carried scientific instruments, including the Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer (APXS). This instrument helped scientists study the chemical composition of Martian rocks and soil. The rover also had cameras that captured images and sent them to Earth through the Pathfinder lander.

04 — THE MISSION

83 Days on Mars

Sojourner was originally designed to operate for only seven days. However, it continued working for 83 days, far exceeding its planned mission duration. During this time, it studied rocks and soil at 16 different locations and travelled approximately 100 meters.

05 — LEGACY

A Small Rover, A Giant Legacy

Sojourner represented a major achievement in space exploration. Its mission helped scientists learn more about Mars and provided valuable experience for designing later rovers. Its success contributed to the development of future explorers such as Spirit, Opportunity and Curiosity. Although its mission ended, its contribution to planetary exploration remains unforgettable.

Abandoned but not Forgotten

Some journeys never truly end.
