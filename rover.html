01 — INTRODUCTION

What Was the Lunar Rover?

The Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), also known as the Moon Buggy, was a small electric vehicle designed by NASA to help astronauts travel across the Moon's surface. It allowed astronauts to explore larger areas, collect lunar rocks and soil, and conduct scientific investigations. The vehicle was used during Apollo 15, Apollo 16 and Apollo 17 between 1971 and 1972.

02 — HISTORIC JOURNEYS

Three Missions, Three Adventures

APOLLO 15 · 1971

The First Lunar Rover

Astronauts David Scott and James Irwin used the first Lunar Rover to explore the Moon. They travelled approximately 27.9 km during three exploration trips.

APOLLO 16 · 1972

Exploring the Descartes Highlands

John Young and Charles Duke drove the Lunar Rover across the Descartes Highlands. They travelled approximately 26.7 km and collected lunar samples.

APOLLO 17 · 1972

The Final Lunar Rover Mission

Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt explored the Taurus-Littrow Valley. They travelled approximately 35.9 km during their lunar exploration.

03 — ENGINEERING

How Did It Work?

The Lunar Rover had a lightweight design that allowed it to operate in the Moon's low-gravity environment.


Electric Motors

Four electric motors powered the vehicle, with one motor for each wheel.


Special Wheels

Four specially designed wire-mesh wheels helped the rover move across the rough lunar surface.


Batteries

Two non-rechargeable batteries supplied electrical power to the vehicle.


Navigation and Steering

The steering and navigation systems helped astronauts control the rover and monitor their journeys.

04 — TECHNICAL FACTS

Meet the Moon Buggy

Maximum speed: Approximately 13 km/h

Number of wheels: 4

Number of astronauts: 2

Power source: Two batteries

Missions: Apollo 15, 16 and 17

05 — THE LEGACY

Still on the Moon

The Lunar Roving Vehicles greatly improved lunar exploration. They helped astronauts travel farther, visit different geological locations and collect more scientific samples. After the missions ended, all three vehicles remained on the Moon, becoming permanent reminders of human exploration.
