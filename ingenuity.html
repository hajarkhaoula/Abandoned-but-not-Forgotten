MARS · NASA · 2021–2024

Ingenuity Mars Helicopter

The first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet.

01 — INTRODUCTION

A New Way to Explore Mars

Ingenuity was a small robotic helicopter developed by NASA to demonstrate that powered flight was possible on another planet. It travelled to Mars attached to the Perseverance rover and landed in Jezero Crater on February 18, 2021.

02 — FIRST FLIGHT

April 19, 2021

Ingenuity made history by completing the first powered, controlled flight on another planet. Its two pairs of fast-spinning rotor blades allowed it to rise above the Martian surface. Flying on Mars was a major engineering challenge because its atmosphere is extremely thin.

03 — DESIGN AND TECHNOLOGY

A Helicopter Built for Another World

Ingenuity was designed to operate in the difficult conditions of Mars. It included:

  • Four carbon-fiber rotor blades arranged in two pairs.
  • Solar panels to recharge its batteries.
  • Navigation cameras and sensors.
  • A lightweight body designed for low gravity.

Its rotor blades had to spin much faster than ordinary helicopter blades on Earth to generate lift in the thin Martian atmosphere.

04 — THE MISSION

72 Flights Instead of Five

Ingenuity was originally designed to complete only five test flights over 30 days. However, it exceeded expectations by completing 72 flights over almost three years. It also helped the Perseverance rover by taking aerial images and observing areas from above.

05 — THE FINAL FLIGHT

January 18, 2024

Ingenuity's final flight took place on January 18, 2024. During its landing, its rotor blades were damaged. NASA officially ended the mission on January 25, 2024. Although it can no longer fly, Ingenuity remains on Mars as a historic technological achievement.

06 — LEGACY

A New Era of Planetary Exploration

Ingenuity proved that powered flight is possible on Mars. Its success opened new possibilities for using flying robots to explore distant worlds in the future. Its mission changed the way scientists imagine exploring other planets.

Abandoned but not Forgotten

Some journeys never truly end.
