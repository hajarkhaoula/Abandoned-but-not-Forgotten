Pioneer 10 & Pioneer 11

Two pioneering spacecraft that explored the outer planets and travelled into the depths of space.

01 — INTRODUCTION

Exploring the Unknown

Pioneer 10 and Pioneer 11 were two robotic spacecraft launched by NASA to explore the outer planets of our solar system. They helped scientists discover important information about Jupiter, Saturn and the space beyond them. Their missions marked a major achievement in the history of space exploration.

02 — PIONEER 10

The First to Jupiter

Pioneer 10 was launched on March 2, 1972. It became the first spacecraft to travel through the asteroid belt and the first to fly past Jupiter, on December 4, 1973. It studied Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field and radiation environment. NASA received its last signal on January 23, 2003.

03 — PIONEER 11

The First Close Look at Saturn

Pioneer 11 was launched on April 6, 1973. It flew past Jupiter in 1974 and became the first spacecraft to study Saturn up close on September 1, 1979. It sent back important information about Saturn's rings, atmosphere and magnetic field. NASA received its last contact in September 1995.

04 — THE PIONEER PLAQUE

A Message from Earth

Both spacecraft carried a special golden plaque designed by Carl Sagan, Frank Drake and Linda Salzman Sagan. The plaque contains illustrations of a human man and woman, a diagram of the Solar System, and information about Earth's location. It was created as a symbolic message to any intelligent life that might discover the spacecraft in the distant future.

05 — LEGACY

Beyond the Solar System

Pioneer 10 and Pioneer 11 opened the way for later explorations of the outer planets. Their discoveries helped prepare the way for the Voyager missions. Although communication with both spacecraft has ended, they continue travelling through space. Their journeys remain an important part of humanity's exploration of the universe.
