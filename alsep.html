01 — INTRODUCTION

What Was ALSEP?

The Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP) was a collection of scientific instruments deployed by astronauts on the Moon during several Apollo missions. These instruments studied the Moon's internal structure, seismic activity, magnetic field, temperature and environment. They continued sending scientific data to Earth after astronauts returned home.

02 — HISTORIC MISSIONS

Scientific Packages on the Moon

APOLLO 11 · 1969

EASEP

Apollo 11 deployed the Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package (EASEP), an early scientific system that included a passive seismometer and a lunar dust detector. A laser retroreflector was also deployed separately.

APOLLO 12 · 1969

The First ALSEP Station

Apollo 12 deployed a more advanced scientific station. Its instruments studied lunar vibrations, magnetic fields, solar wind particles and the lunar environment.

APOLLO 15 · 1971

An Expanded Scientific Station

Apollo 15 deployed another ALSEP station with instruments including a heat flow experiment, a magnetometer and a seismometer.

03 — SCIENTIFIC INSTRUMENTS

What Did They Study?

Passive Seismometer

Detected vibrations and seismic activity on the Moon, helping scientists understand its internal structure.


Lunar Surface Magnetometer

Measured magnetic fields near the lunar surface.


Solar Wind Spectrometer

Studied charged particles coming from the Sun.


Heat Flow Experiment

Measured heat moving from the Moon's interior toward its surface.


Laser Retroreflector

Reflected laser signals sent from Earth, allowing scientists to measure the distance between Earth and the Moon with great precision.

04 — POWER AND COMMUNICATION

How Did ALSEP Work?

The ALSEP stations used a central control unit to supply power, manage experiments and transmit scientific information to Earth.


Apollo 12 and Apollo 15 used the SNAP-27 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG). It converted heat from radioactive decay of Plutonium-238 into electrical energy.


Apollo 11's EASEP used solar power instead.

05 — THE END OF AN ERA

September 30, 1977

The Apollo ALSEP stations continued transmitting scientific data for several years after the missions. NASA officially shut down the ALSEP stations on September 30, 1977. Their instruments remain on the Moon as reminders of a remarkable scientific achievement.
